Privacy Policy

Radiova respects your privacy. The public site does not use cookies, advertising cookies, third-party analytics, or tracking. After consent, app data is stored locally in your browser using localStorage, IndexedDB, and Cache Storage.

What data we store

  • Your favorite stations
  • Listen history
  • App settings (language, theme, volume)
  • Custom playlists
  • Offline app shell and playlist cache

Third-party services

Radiova fetches station data from the open radiova-stations GitHub repository, and this site is hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub receives those requests. Stream audio and station logos are loaded directly from the stations' own servers, which see your IP address and browser details like any website you visit; Radiova does not receive or relay these requests. No third-party analytics or tracking is used.

Browser extension

The Radiova browser extension for Chrome, Edge, and Firefox keeps its data (favorites, station lists, settings, a cached copy of the station catalog, and local listening statistics) in the extension's own storage in your browser. It contacts the same station catalog on GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com), the stations' own servers when you play or test a station, and, only when you use Discover, the GitHub repository you choose (api.github.com and raw.githubusercontent.com); these servers see your IP address like any website. To pause or mute the radio when a call or other audio starts, it reads which tabs are playing sound, with their address and title, on your device only; this is never stored or sent. The extension has no analytics and shows no ads.

Signing in with Google (optional)

Signing in is optional. Playback, favorites, playlists, and settings work without an account. Until you press "Sign in with Google", this site does not contact Google or Firebase for sign-in. If you signed in on an earlier visit and allowed browser storage, the site contacts Firebase on later visits to restore that session.

When you sign in, your browser connects to Google and Firebase servers and opens a Google window. Google tells Radiova's Firebase project your name, email address, profile picture address, and a user ID. Radiova shows only your name or email in the top bar and does not load your picture.

Firebase keeps your sign-in session in your browser's storage (IndexedDB or localStorage) so that you stay signed in after a restart; in privacy mode the session lasts only until you close the tab. After you accept storage, Radiova also stores a single flag saying that you were signed in; it contains no personal data. Radiova uses your name, email address, and user ID only to show who is signed in and to find your own data in your account.

While you are signed in and have accepted browser storage, Radiova copies two kinds of data to your account in Google Firebase (Firestore): your favorite stations and a few settings (currently the volume level). Other browsers where you sign in with the same account show the same favorites. The database rules allow only your own account to read or change them. Custom playlists, recently played stations, imported or exported files, and cached playlists are never copied and stay in this browser. In privacy mode nothing is synced. When favorites differ between devices they are merged: a station added anywhere is kept, and a station you remove on one device is removed on the others.

The browser extension uses the same optional Google sign-in and the same account: Chrome and Edge open Google's sign-in window through the browser's identity feature, and Firefox first asks for its built-in permission to share this data. The extension copies your favorites and most of its settings to your account, and has its own "Delete account" button in the dashboard. The sign-in session is kept in the extension's browser storage. In Chrome and Edge, Google's answer passes through one page of this site (radiova-app.github.io/auth/extension/) inside your browser; the page only hands it to the extension: it stores nothing and sends it to no server.

Google runs the sign-in service and the database (Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore) that Radiova uses. Your account record and the copy of your favorites and settings stay there until you delete your account. Radiova does not sell this data, does not use it for advertising or analytics, and shows no ads.

Signing out ends the session and stops syncing in this browser; it deletes nothing from this browser or from your account. "Reset all data" and "Withdraw consent and clear data" remove the local data and the stored session from this browser and stop syncing; they do not delete the copy in your account. Signing out does not delete your account record at Google and Firebase either, and to delete your account together with the copy of your favorites and settings, use "Delete account" in the account menu while you are signed in.
